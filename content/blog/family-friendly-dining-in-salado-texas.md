Family-friendly dining in Salado, Texas means a handful of addresses built for a table with kids in it: a steakhouse-and-barbecue hall with a stage out back, a burger stand that still hand-dips its milkshakes, a bakery that opens before the shops do, and a crêperie where the show is watching breakfast get made. None of the four asks a parent to choose between a good meal and a patient waiter.

## Johnny's Steaks & Bar-Be-Que

Johnny's Steaks & Bar-Be-Que sits at 301 Thomas Arnold Road, just off I-35 at Exit 284 on the south end of the village — a short drive rather than a walk from Main Street (City of Salado, 2026). Father-and-son owners Johnny and Josh Bratton, both Texas A&M graduates, opened the Salado location in 2002, a decade after Johnny started Johnny's Texas Style Bar-Be-Que in Killeen (Tex Appeal Magazine, 2024). The menu runs mesquite-grilled steaks cut in-house — a 12-ounce ribeye among them — alongside pit barbecue: brisket, sausage, and ribs, plus a "classic tailgate special" built for a crowd.

What makes it a family stop rather than just a dinner stop is the room itself: an indoor dining area built for groups, an outdoor cantina, and Johnny's Outback, an events space that has hosted country acts including Tracy Lawrence, Eddy Raven, John Conlee, and Asleep at the Wheel, plus Game Day watch parties during football season (Tex Appeal Magazine, 2024). Co-owner Josh Bratton has described the goal as a place where "everyone feels at home," and the Brattons' support for the Salado schools' science fair points to a restaurant that treats the town's kids as regulars, not an afterthought.

## Old Fashioned Burgers & Ice Cream

At 882 N. Main St., Old Fashioned Burgers & Ice Cream is exactly what the name promises: classic burgers and hand-dipped milkshakes, open limited days — Tuesday and Friday from 7am to 5pm, Saturday from 11am to 6pm, closed the rest of the week (Visit Salado, 2026). It is the smallest and least fussy of the four, built for a lunch stop between the creek and the shops rather than a planned evening, and the kind of order a six-year-old can make without help.

## McCain's Bakery & Café

McCain's Bakery & Café, at 417 N. Main St., is a family-owned bakery and café serving scratch-made breakfast and lunch, fresh pastries, and locally roasted coffee in a cozy, unhurried room (Salado Chamber of Commerce, 2026). It is the stop that works before a day in the village has really started — a pastry and coffee for the adults, something sweet for the kids, before Main Street's shops open.

## Marketplace Café & Crêperie

Inside the Shoppes on Main at 22 N. Main St. — a single building that holds more than twenty-five boutiques — Marketplace Café & Crêperie makes its crêpes fresh to order on a 16-inch skillet in view of the counter, sweet with Nutella and fruit or savory with spinach and cheese, alongside handcrafted coffee built around a cold-brew specialty (Visit Salado, 2026). Watching the crêpe get made is most of the entertainment for a younger kid, and the shops around it give everyone else somewhere to wander while the table waits.

## What family-friendly dining tells a buyer

Four kitchens built around groups rather than couples is not the whole of Salado's dining scene — the village's cocktail bar, brewery, and steakhouse-by-the-creek cover the other half, detailed in the [guide to where to eat and drink in Salado](/blog/where-to-eat-and-drink-in-salado-texas) — but it is the half that matters most to a family actually moving here. A kid who can order a milkshake at Old Fashioned Burgers, a stage at Johnny's Outback for a Friday night, and a crêpe counter inside a building full of shops adds up to less a list of restaurants than a description of what a Saturday looks like once the moving boxes are gone. It is also a reason families relocating for Fort Cavazos or Temple's medical corridor look at [Salado](/neighborhoods#salado) as more than a weekend trip — the dining options read as a town built for a whole household, not just the two adults signing the mortgage. ## Frequently asked questions

### What restaurants in Salado, Texas are good for families with kids?

Johnny's Steaks & Bar-Be-Que, Old Fashioned Burgers & Ice Cream, McCain's Bakery & Café, and Marketplace Café & Crêperie all welcome kids, ranging from a sit-down barbecue dinner with live music to a quick burger and milkshake on Main Street.

### Is Johnny's Steaks & Bar-Be-Que in Salado kid-friendly?

Yes. The restaurant has an indoor dining room built for groups and a long history of multigenerational customers, and its owners describe the goal as making every guest feel at home (Tex Appeal Magazine, 2024).

### Where can kids get ice cream or a quick bite in Salado?

Old Fashioned Burgers & Ice Cream at 882 N. Main St. serves classic burgers and hand-dipped milkshakes, though its hours are limited to Tuesday, Friday, and Saturday (Visit Salado, 2026).

### Are there casual breakfast spots in Salado for families?

McCain's Bakery & Café on N. Main St. serves scratch-made breakfast, pastries, and coffee in a family-owned, unhurried setting, and Marketplace Café & Crêperie inside the Shoppes on Main serves sweet and savory crêpes made to order (Salado Chamber of Commerce, 2026; Visit Salado, 2026).

### How far is Johnny's Steaks & Bar-Be-Que from Fort Cavazos?

Johnny's sits roughly 30 minutes from Fort Cavazos by car, making it a common stop for military families based there, according to coverage in the Fort Hood Sentinel (Fort Hood Sentinel, 2026).
