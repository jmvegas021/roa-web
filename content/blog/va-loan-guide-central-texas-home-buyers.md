A VA loan lets an eligible service member, veteran, or surviving spouse buy a home with no down payment and no monthly mortgage insurance — and in Bell and Williamson Counties, where the standard 2026 limit sits at $832,750, that benefit covers the overwhelming majority of homes on the market without a down payment at all (The Military Wallet, 2026). What trips up Central Texas buyers isn't the loan itself; it's the handful of rules — entitlement, funding fee tiers, and which fees a veteran simply isn't allowed to pay — that a generic mortgage explainer skips.

## Who actually qualifies

Eligibility runs on service time, not branch or rank. Active-duty service members generally qualify after 90 consecutive days of active service during wartime or 181 days during peacetime; National Guard and Reserve members typically need more than six years of service, or 90 days of active service under Title 32 with at least 30 of those consecutive; and surviving spouses of a service member who died in the line of duty or from a service-connected disability may also be eligible (The Mortgage Reports, 2026). None of that requires a specific credit score by VA rule — the VA sets no minimum — though lenders set their own floor, commonly around 620 and occasionally as low as 580, and look for no more than one 30-day late payment in the past twelve months (The Mortgage Reports, 2026).

## Entitlement and the 2026 loan limit

Since federal rules changed the program, a veteran with full entitlement has no VA loan limit at all — a lender can approve any amount the borrower qualifies for with zero down. The limit matters only for a buyer with reduced or partial entitlement, typically someone carrying an existing VA loan or who defaulted on a prior one. For 2026, the standard limit is $832,750, up 3.3% from $806,500 in 2025, and neither Bell County nor Williamson County appears on the list of higher-cost counties, so both fall under that standard figure (The Military Wallet, 2026; Veterans United, 2026). A reduced-entitlement borrower can still finance above that number — the math just requires a down payment on the portion above the zero-down ceiling, calculated from the entitlement actually remaining.

## The funding fee, and which closing costs a buyer can skip

The VA funding fee replaces mortgage insurance and scales with down payment and prior use. On a first-time use with no down payment, it runs 2.3% of the loan amount; a subsequent use at zero down runs 3.6%; putting down 5–10% drops the fee to 1.65% regardless of prior use, and 10% or more brings it to 1.4% (The Mortgage Reports, 2026). Veterans and service members receiving VA compensation for a service-connected disability, Purple Heart recipients on active duty, and qualifying surviving spouses are exempt from the fee entirely — a detail worth confirming on the Certificate of Eligibility before a lender builds it into the loan estimate.

VA rules also cap or bar other costs outright when a lender charges the alternative flat 1% origination fee: a buyer cannot be charged loan application or processing fees, lender inspection fees (outside VA construction loans), broker or trustee fees, escrow or notary fees, rate lock-in fees, document preparation fees, a lender's own appraisal fee, postage, tax service fees, or separate settlement fees — those either get absorbed by the lender or shift to the seller (PenFed, 2026). Sellers can also contribute concessions — covering the funding fee, prepaid taxes and insurance, or paying off a buyer's small debts — up to a combined 4% of the home's appraised value, though nothing obligates a seller to offer them (PenFed, 2026).

## Requesting the Certificate of Eligibility

The Certificate of Eligibility, or COE, is what turns a lender's interest into an actual pre-approval, and the fastest route is usually through the lender itself, which can often pull eligibility electronically once a borrower's service record is on file. Applying directly online can return the COE immediately in some cases; a mailed paper application typically takes four to six weeks, with the VA aiming to respond within five business days of receiving a request (Rocket Mortgage, 2026). Requesting it before touring homes — not after an offer — is what keeps a Central Texas purchase on a normal timeline instead of a delayed one.

## Where the benefit goes furthest locally

A zero-down VA purchase works the same way on a creek-side lot in Salado as it does on a subdivision street in the [Harker Heights](/neighborhoods#harker-heights) corridor — the loan doesn't discriminate by town, only by price against entitlement. For most buyers working within the $832,750 standard limit, Belton, Temple, and Harker Heights are all realistically in reach with no down payment at all, which is worth knowing before ruling out a neighborhood on price assumptions alone. ## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a down payment to use a VA loan in Texas?

No. Eligible borrowers with full entitlement can finance up to 100% of the purchase price with no down payment, regardless of the home's location in Bell or Williamson County. A down payment only becomes relevant for a reduced-entitlement borrower financing above the zero-down ceiling.

### What credit score do I need for a VA loan?

The VA itself sets no minimum credit score. Individual lenders set their own requirements, commonly around 620, with some approving borrowers as low as 580 depending on the rest of the credit file (The Mortgage Reports, 2026).

### How much is the VA funding fee in 2026?

On a first-time, zero-down use it's 2.3% of the loan amount; a subsequent zero-down use runs 3.6%. Any down payment of 5% or more lowers the fee to 1.65% or 1.4%. Veterans with a service-connected disability rating, Purple Heart recipients, and qualifying surviving spouses are exempt (The Mortgage Reports, 2026).

### Is there a maximum VA loan amount in Bell County?

Not for a borrower with full entitlement — there's no cap. For a reduced-entitlement borrower, the 2026 standard limit of $832,750 applies, since Bell County isn't on the higher-cost county list (The Military Wallet, 2026).

### Can a seller help cover my VA loan closing costs?

Yes, within limits. Seller concessions — covering the funding fee, prepaid costs, or small debts — can total up to 4% of the home's appraised value, though a seller isn't required to offer any (PenFed, 2026).
