Where to eat and drink in Salado, Texas comes down to four addresses within a few blocks of the creek: a cocktail bar on Main Street, a brewery on Royal Street, a steakhouse at the water's edge, and a dining room that has fed travelers since 1947. None of the four requires a car once you're parked in the village, and together they cover most of what a weekend visit — or a Tuesday night, for people who live here — actually asks for.

## Bar 1859

Bar 1859 sits at 21 N. Main St., named for the founding year of Salado College, according to the bar's own history (Bar 1859, 2026). Owners Chuck and Tracey Eddy built it as a relaxed-but-upscale gathering spot: handcrafted cocktails, a bourbon bar with private spirit lockers for regulars, and a wine list, in a room meant for lingering rather than rushing through a tab. It is a stop suited to an evening walk down Main Street rather than a full sit-down dinner — think a pre- or post-dinner drink, or the whole night if conversation is the point. Hours run 3:30pm to 10pm Monday through Wednesday, later Thursday and Friday, and from noon on Saturday and Sunday.

## Barrow Brewing Company

A few blocks over at 108 Royal St., Barrow Brewing Company is the only brewery in Salado and the first microbrewery in Bell County (Barrow Brewing Company, 2026). The taproom opens onto a creek-side beer garden and a field that doubles as an events lawn, and since the kitchen is a rotating cast of food trucks — Sacred Sandwich, Happy Pizza Company's woodfired pies, Longbow Coffee, and Far Out 54 for sno cones — there is almost always something to eat alongside the beer. The brewery is deliberately family- and pet-friendly: free water and non-alcoholic drinks for kids, leashed dogs welcome, and a "puppy tap" for the ones who make the trip. Live music plays most weekends, and at least one food truck is on site daily.

## Alexander's Craft Cocktails & Kitchen

Alexander's occupies a historic building at 602 Center Cir, described on its own listing as "hidden at the edge of Salado Creek" (Visit Salado, 2026). It is the closest of the four to a proper dinner reservation — steak and seafood on the menu, craft cocktails built to match the food rather than stand alone — and the creek-side setting makes it a favorite for a slower evening rather than a quick stop between shops. Where Bar 1859 reads as a Main Street bar and Barrow Brewing reads as an outdoor taproom, Alexander's reads as the village's dinner-and-a-drink destination.

## Stagecoach Inn

The Stagecoach Inn is the village's oldest continuously operating hotel, built in the 1860s as the Salado Hotel and known for decades as the Shady Villa before taking its current name when the restaurant opened in 1947 (Stagecoach Inn, 2026). Guests along the old Chisholm Trail included Sam Houston, Robert E. Lee, and Jesse James, and the property earned Texas Historical Landmark status in 1962. Its hushpuppies have stayed on the menu since that 1947 opening night, born out of necessity when the kitchen had no set menu and started frying what it had on hand — the recipe was released to the public in 1963, deliberately altered to protect the original. The restaurant closed for a two-year renovation and reopened under new ownership in 2024, now serving lunch and dinner Wednesday through Sunday, 11:30am to 8pm, with the hushpuppies still on the table alongside newer dishes like a goat cheese dip, fried chicken salad, a grilled bone-in pork chop with peach chutney, and a weekend prime rib special (CultureMap Austin, 2026).

## What the dining scene tells a buyer

Four kitchens within walking distance of each other is not an accident of a big city — it is what [Salado](/neighborhoods#salado)'s scale does on purpose. A creek-side village built around a mile of Main Street keeps its restaurants, galleries, and tasting rooms close enough that an evening does not require a car, and close enough that regulars end up recognizing each other across all four rooms. That is part of what a buyer is actually purchasing when they choose Salado over a larger neighbor: a walk to dinner, not just a house on a larger lot. ## Frequently asked questions

### What is the oldest restaurant in Salado, Texas?

The Stagecoach Inn is the oldest, operating from a hotel building constructed in the 1860s. The restaurant itself opened under that name in 1947 and has kept its original hushpuppies on the menu since (Stagecoach Inn, 2026).

### Is there a brewery in Salado, Texas?

Yes. Barrow Brewing Company at 108 Royal St. is the only brewery in Salado and the first microbrewery in Bell County, with a creek-side beer garden and rotating food trucks rather than a kitchen of its own (Barrow Brewing Company, 2026).

### Are Bar 1859 and Alexander's within walking distance of each other?

Bar 1859 is on Main Street and Alexander's is a short walk away at 602 Center Cir near the creek, with Barrow Brewing on Royal Street in between — all three sit inside Salado's compact, walkable village core.

### Does Salado have restaurants that take dinner reservations?

Alexander's Craft Cocktails & Kitchen and the Stagecoach Inn are the two best suited to a planned dinner, with steak, seafood, and a full bar. Bar 1859 and Barrow Brewing lean more toward a casual drink or a food-truck plate.

### What are the Stagecoach Inn's hushpuppies known for?

They have been on the menu since the restaurant's 1947 opening, created when the kitchen had no set menu and improvised with what was on hand. The recipe was publicly released in 1963, though intentionally altered to keep the original version exclusive to the Inn (Stagecoach Inn, 2026).
