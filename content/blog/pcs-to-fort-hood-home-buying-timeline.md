A PCS to Fort Hood comes with a report date and little else — the home-buying timeline has to be built backward from that date, and it starts with a VA Certificate of Eligibility, not a signed contract. Families who begin that paperwork the week orders arrive routinely close before their household goods do; families who wait for a house to fall in love with first are usually still waiting on financing after the moving truck has already unloaded into a rental.

## Start the file before you have an address

The Certificate of Eligibility, or COE, is the document that tells a lender you qualify for a VA-backed loan, and it can be requested long before you have an address in mind. Active-duty members request it with a statement of service signed by their commander, adjutant, or personnel officer; veterans use a DD214; a lender can often pull it directly through the VA's Web LGY system rather than waiting on a mailed form, which the agency notes takes longer, according to VA.gov (2026). None of this requires leave, a trip to Texas, or a ratified contract — it is paperwork that can move in parallel with everything else on a PCS checklist.

That head start matters because the household-goods calendar does not wait. Permanent change-of-station season peaks from June through August, and the Army's own transportation guidance warns that waiting until 30 days or less before a move date can leave preferred moving companies unavailable, with pickup and delivery windows running four to six weeks longer in the busiest months, per Army.mil (2022). A VA appraisal and underwriting review take real time of their own; a family racing both a closing date and a household-goods pickup date is better served by having already cleared the financing step before travel orders are cut. Lenders order the appraisal once the COE and a purchase contract are in hand — it values the property but is not a substitute for an inspection, a distinction worth keeping in mind on a compressed timeline.

## Why Harker Heights, specifically

Harker Heights sits immediately outside Fort Hood's eastern gates and has grown alongside the post for decades — its population reached 33,097 at the 2020 census, up from 17,308 in 2000, with a 2023 estimate near 34,447, according to the U.S. Census Bureau (2023). That growth shows up as a wider, steadier supply of three- and four-bedroom homes than a family will find inside Killeen proper, without trading away the short commute. [Homes near Fort Hood in Harker Heights](/neighborhoods#harker-heights) tend to read as conventional, well-kept suburban stock rather than a single master-planned product — useful when a PCS leaves little room to be particular about floor plan, only about closing date.

## Schools without a second zoning decision

Killeen Independent School District serves Harker Heights, Killeen, Nolanville, and children living on the Fort Hood reservation itself, which means a family does not have to weigh a separate municipal school system on top of the base-versus-civilian housing decision, per Military OneSource's MilitaryINSTALLATIONS directory (2026). Assignment is zoned by home address, with a transfer request process available through the district if a specific campus matters more than the address does — worth confirming directly with KISD Student Services before a contract is signed, since boundary lines shift and online directories lag reality.

## Buy now or rent first — the actual decision point

The honest answer depends on how firm the report date is and how the market in Harker Heights and the surrounding Bell County corridor is moving that season, not on a rule of thumb. A family with a confirmed report date more than 60 days out, a COE already in hand, and a realistic sense of neighborhoods has room to buy on arrival rather than sign a lease. A family compressing a house hunt into a single weekend of leave is usually better served renting for a season and buying with eyes open once they are living the commute, the schools, and the drive to the commissary rather than guessing at them from a previous duty station. Either path can end in ownership; only one of them risks a rushed offer on a street you have never actually driven at 7 a.m.

## What not to rush, even on a tight calendar

A compressed timeline is a reason to start early, not a reason to skip steps. Keep the inspection, even on a home that appraised cleanly — appraisal and inspection answer different questions. Keep the walk of the actual commute route to the gate you will use, at the hour you will use it, rather than trusting a map's estimate. And keep a second set of eyes, Kevin's office among them, reviewing the contract terms that a PCS clock tends to make people skip past. The families who land well at Fort Hood are rarely the ones who moved fastest; they are the ones who started earliest.

## Frequently asked questions

### How early should I start the VA loan process before a PCS to Fort Hood?

As soon as orders arrive. Request the Certificate of Eligibility immediately — it requires no contract or trip to Texas — and let the underwriting and appraisal steps run in parallel with your household-goods scheduling rather than after it, per VA.gov (2026).

### Is Harker Heights inside Fort Hood's commute range?

Yes. Harker Heights borders the post's eastern side and has grown directly alongside it since the 1950s, which keeps most of the city within a short daily commute to the gates, according to the U.S. Census Bureau (2023).

### Do I need to pick a specific school before buying a home near Fort Hood?

Not necessarily. Killeen ISD serves Harker Heights, Killeen, Nolanville, and the Fort Hood reservation under one district, with address-based zoning and a transfer process if a particular campus matters more than the street does, per Military OneSource (2026).

### Should I rent in Harker Heights before buying, if my report date is tight?

If your report date leaves only days of house-hunting leave, renting a season first is often the more honest choice than rushing an offer. If you already have a COE in hand and more than about two months before report date, buying on arrival is realistic.

### What's the biggest timeline mistake incoming families make?

Treating the house hunt as the first step instead of the financing. The COE, lender preapproval, and a sense of the commute should all be settled before serious touring begins — not discovered during it.
